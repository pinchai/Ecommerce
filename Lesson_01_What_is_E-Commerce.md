# Lesson 01 — What is E-Commerce?

**Course:** E-Commerce  
**Lesson:** 01  
**Duration:** 2 Hours  
**Level:** Beginner  
**Teaching Approach:** Concept → Examples → Discussion → Activity → Review

---

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Define **E-Commerce (Electronic Commerce)**.
2. Explain how e-commerce is different from traditional commerce.
3. Identify common examples of e-commerce in everyday life.
4. Explain the basic e-commerce transaction process.
5. Identify major types of e-commerce: **B2C, B2B, C2C, C2B, B2G, C2G, and G2G**.
6. Recognize the major components required for an e-commerce system.
7. Explain basic advantages and challenges of e-commerce.
8. Classify real-world businesses according to e-commerce models.

---

# 2. Opening Discussion

Start the lesson with these questions:

- Have you ever bought something using a phone?
- Where did you buy it?
- Did you pay online or when the product arrived?
- Have you ever sold something online?
- What happens after you click **Buy Now**?

### Teacher's Key Idea

Students already use e-commerce in daily life. The goal of this lesson is to understand **what happens behind an online transaction**, not just how to shop online.

---

# 3. What is Commerce?

**Commerce** is the exchange of goods and services between buyers and sellers.

A simple commerce process is:

```text
Seller
   ↓
Product / Service
   ↓
Customer
   ↓
Payment
```

### Example

A customer goes to a clothing shop:

```text
Customer → Select Shirt → Pay Money → Receive Shirt
```

This is **traditional commerce** because the transaction happens through a physical location.

---

# 4. What is E-Commerce?

## Definition

**E-Commerce (Electronic Commerce)** is the buying and selling of goods or services through electronic networks, especially the Internet.

It allows customers and businesses to perform commercial transactions using:

- Websites
- Mobile applications
- Online marketplaces
- Social commerce platforms
- Digital payment systems

A customer may browse products, place an order, make a payment, and receive the product or service without visiting a physical store.

> **E-Commerce = Commercial transactions conducted electronically, primarily through the Internet.**

The course reference also emphasizes that e-commerce is broader than simply having an online store; digital technologies can transform how businesses interact with customers and conduct transactions.

---

# 5. Simple Example of E-Commerce

Suppose a customer wants to buy a pair of shoes.

### Traditional Commerce

```text
Customer
   ↓
Travel to Store
   ↓
Look at Shoes
   ↓
Choose Product
   ↓
Pay
   ↓
Take Shoes Home
```

### E-Commerce

```text
Customer
   ↓
Website / Mobile App
   ↓
Browse Products
   ↓
Add to Cart
   ↓
Checkout
   ↓
Online Payment
   ↓
Order Processing
   ↓
Delivery
   ↓
Customer Receives Product
```

The important difference is that the **commercial transaction is enabled through digital technology**.

# 6. E-Commerce in Everyday Life

Common examples include:

- Buying clothes from an online store
- Ordering food using a mobile app
- Buying electronics from an online marketplace
- Booking a hotel online
- Buying airline tickets
- Paying for digital subscriptions
- Buying software or online courses
- Selling used products through an online marketplace
- Hiring freelancers through an online platform

### Think About It

Ask students:

> "If I order food from my phone and pay digitally, is that e-commerce?"

**Answer:** Yes. It is an online commercial transaction involving a customer, a business/service provider, payment, and fulfillment.

---

# 7. E-Commerce vs Traditional Commerce

| Traditional Commerce | E-Commerce |
|---|---|
| Physical store | Website / App / Marketplace |
| Face-to-face interaction | Digital interaction |
| Usually limited by store hours | Can operate 24/7 |
| Customers often visit the store | Customers can shop remotely |
| Cash/card at physical location | Online and digital payment options |
| Local reach may be limited | Can reach wider markets |
| Paper or local records | Digital records |
| Physical product inspection | Product information, images, reviews |
| Immediate physical possession | Delivery may be required |

### Important Point

E-commerce does **not** mean that every part of a business must be online.

For example:

```text
Online Order
     ↓
Online Payment
     ↓
Physical Warehouse
     ↓
Physical Delivery
     ↓
Customer
```

The transaction can be digital while fulfillment still involves physical operations.

# 8. How Does E-Commerce Work?

A basic e-commerce transaction can be represented as:

```text
Customer
   │
   ▼
Website / Mobile App
   │
   ▼
Product Catalog
   │
   ▼
Shopping Cart
   │
   ▼
Checkout
   │
   ▼
Payment Gateway
   │
   ▼
Order Management
   │
   ▼
Warehouse / Seller
   │
   ▼
Delivery
   │
   ▼
Customer
```

## Step-by-Step

### Step 1 — Customer discovers a product

The customer searches for or discovers a product through:

- Search engines
- Social media
- Advertisements
- Marketplace search
- Direct website visits

### Step 2 — Customer views the product

The product page may contain:

- Product name
- Images
- Description
- Price
- Stock status
- Reviews
- Delivery information

### Step 3 — Customer adds the product to the cart

The shopping cart stores the products selected by the customer before checkout.

### Step 4 — Checkout

The customer provides information such as:

- Name
- Delivery address
- Phone number
- Payment method

### Step 5 — Payment

The payment system processes the transaction.

Examples:

- Credit/debit cards
- Bank transfer
- Digital wallets
- QR payments
- Cash on delivery

### Step 6 — Order processing

The business receives and processes the order.

### Step 7 — Fulfillment

The product is:

```text
Warehouse → Packing → Shipping → Delivery
```

### Step 8 — Customer receives the product

The order is completed when the product or service is delivered.

# 9. Major Types of E-Commerce

The most common models are based on **who is doing business with whom**.

```text
B = Business
C = Consumer
G = Government
```

---

## 9.1 B2C — Business to Consumer

A **business sells directly to individual consumers**.

```text
Business → Consumer
```

### Examples

- Online clothing store
- Electronics store
- Online grocery store
- Online retailer

### Example

```text
Online Store
     ↓
Customer
```

This is one of the easiest e-commerce models for students to recognize.

---

## 9.2 B2B — Business to Business

A **business sells products or services to another business**.

```text
Business → Business
```

### Examples

- Manufacturer selling to a distributor
- Wholesale supplier selling to retailers
- Cloud software sold to companies
- Business procurement platforms

### Example

```text
Manufacturer
      ↓
Distributor
      ↓
Retailer
```

B2B e-commerce can involve procurement, supply chains, wholesale transactions, and long-term business relationships.

---

## 9.3 C2C — Consumer to Consumer

A **consumer sells to another consumer**, usually through a platform.

```text
Consumer → Consumer
```

### Examples

- Selling used phones online
- Selling used furniture
- Online marketplaces
- Online auction platforms

### Example

```text
Person A
   ↓
Marketplace
   ↓
Person B
```

The marketplace may provide search, messaging, payment, ratings, and other services.

---

## 9.4 C2B — Consumer to Business

An **individual provides value to a business**.

```text
Consumer → Business
```

### Examples

- Freelancer provides a service to a company
- Photographer licenses an image to a company
- Influencer promotes a business
- Freelancer develops software for a company

### Example

```text
Freelancer
     ↓
Service
     ↓
Company
```

---

## 9.5 B2G — Business to Government

A **business provides products or services to a government organization**.

```text
Business → Government
```

### Examples

- Software company providing systems to a government department
- Contractor submitting a government bid online
- Supplier selling equipment to government agencies

---

## 9.6 C2G — Consumer to Government

A **citizen interacts electronically with government services**.

```text
Citizen → Government
```

### Examples

- Paying government fees online
- Online tax-related services
- Submitting government applications electronically

---

## 9.7 G2G — Government to Government

Electronic transactions or information exchange between government organizations.

```text
Government → Government
```

### Examples

- Government departments sharing information
- Digital administrative systems
- Electronic data exchange between agencies

---

# 10. Summary of E-Commerce Models

| Model | Meaning | Simple Example |
|---|---|---|
| B2C | Business → Consumer | Online retail store |
| B2B | Business → Business | Wholesale supplier |
| C2C | Consumer → Consumer | Used-product marketplace |
| C2B | Consumer → Business | Freelancer → Company |
| B2G | Business → Government | Government supplier |
| C2G | Consumer → Government | Citizen online service |
| G2G | Government → Government | Government data exchange |

### Memory Trick

```text
B = Business
C = Consumer
G = Government

B2C = Business sells to Customer
B2B = Business sells to Business
C2C = Customer sells to Customer
C2B = Customer provides value to Business
```

# 11. Core Components of an E-Commerce System

An e-commerce system normally contains several important components.

## 11.1 Product Catalog

Stores and displays products or services.

```text
Product
├── Name
├── Price
├── Description
├── Image
├── Stock
└── Category
```

---

## 11.2 Shopping Cart

Allows customers to collect products before checkout.

```text
Cart
├── Product A × 2
├── Product B × 1
└── Total
```

---

## 11.3 Checkout

Collects the information required to complete an order.

---

## 11.4 Payment Gateway

Processes electronic payments.

Examples include:

- Card payment
- Digital wallet
- Bank payment
- QR payment

> The payment gateway connects the e-commerce application with a payment service.

---

## 11.5 Order Management System

Tracks the lifecycle of an order:

```text
Pending
   ↓
Paid
   ↓
Processing
   ↓
Shipped
   ↓
Delivered
```

---

## 11.6 Customer Service

Helps customers with:

- Questions
- Returns
- Refunds
- Complaints
- Order problems

---

## 11.7 Security

Security protects:

- Customer information
- Passwords
- Payment information
- Orders
- Business data

Common technologies include:

- HTTPS
- SSL/TLS
- Authentication
- Authorization
- Encryption

# 12. Advantages of E-Commerce

## For Customers

### 1. Convenience

Customers can shop without visiting a physical store.

### 2. 24/7 Availability

Online stores can accept orders outside traditional business hours.

### 3. More Choices

Customers can compare products from many sellers.

### 4. Easy Price Comparison

Customers can compare prices quickly.

### 5. Reviews

Customer reviews can help buyers make decisions.

---

## For Businesses

### 1. Wider Market Reach

A business can reach customers outside its local area.

### 2. Lower Physical Store Costs

Businesses may reduce some costs associated with physical stores.

### 3. Data Collection

Digital systems can provide information about:

- Customers
- Products
- Orders
- Sales
- Marketing

### 4. Automation

Many processes can be automated:

```text
Order
  ↓
Payment
  ↓
Invoice
  ↓
Inventory Update
  ↓
Notification
```

### 5. Personalization

Digital systems can recommend products based on customer behavior.

---

# 13. Challenges of E-Commerce

E-commerce also has disadvantages and risks.

## 13.1 Security and Privacy

Businesses must protect customer information and transactions.

## 13.2 Trust

Customers cannot always physically inspect a product before buying.

## 13.3 Delivery

Physical products must still be packed, shipped, and delivered.

## 13.4 Technical Problems

Examples:

- Website downtime
- Payment failure
- Network problems
- Application bugs

## 13.5 Cybersecurity

E-commerce systems can face threats such as:

- Account attacks
- Payment fraud
- Data breaches
- Malware
- Phishing

## 13.6 Competition

Customers can compare many businesses easily, increasing competition.

# 15. E-Commerce vs E-Business

These terms are related but not identical.

| E-Commerce | E-Business |
|---|---|
| Focuses on electronic buying and selling | Covers broader digital business activities |
| Customer transactions | Transactions + internal operations |
| Online orders and payments | Marketing, HR, inventory, CRM, supply chain, etc. |
| Narrower concept | Broader concept |

### Key Relationship

```text
E-Business
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│ Marketing                   │
│ Human Resources             │
│ Supply Chain                │
│ Inventory                   │
│ Customer Service            │
│ Finance                     │
│                             │
│   ┌─────────────────────┐   │
│   │    E-Commerce       │   │
│   │ Buying & Selling    │   │
│   └─────────────────────┘   │
└─────────────────────────────┘
```

> **E-Commerce is an important part of E-Business.**

# 16. Mini Case Study — Online Clothing Store

Imagine a Cambodian clothing business creates an online store.

### Customer

A customer opens the website and selects a shirt.

### Product Catalog

The website displays:

```text
Product: T-Shirt
Price: $10
Stock: 25
```

### Shopping Cart

```text
T-Shirt × 2
Subtotal: $20
```

### Checkout

The customer enters:

```text
Name
Phone
Address
Payment Method
```

### Payment

The customer pays electronically.

### Order

The system creates:

```text
Order #1001
Status: Paid
Total: $20
```

### Fulfillment

```text
Warehouse
    ↓
Packing
    ↓
Delivery
    ↓
Customer
```

### Discussion

Ask students:

1. Where is the e-commerce transaction?
2. What information is stored in the database?
3. Which system processes payment?
4. Which system manages the order?
5. What happens if payment succeeds but the order is not created?
6. What happens if the product is out of stock?

These questions introduce students to the technical problems they will encounter later in the course.

# 17. Classroom Activity — Identify the E-Commerce Model

Divide students into groups.

Give each group these scenarios:

### Scenario A

An online shop sells a laptop directly to a student.

**Answer:** B2C

### Scenario B

A manufacturer sells 500 phones to a retailer.

**Answer:** B2B

### Scenario C

A student sells a used laptop to another student through an online marketplace.

**Answer:** C2C

### Scenario D

A freelance developer builds a website for a company.

**Answer:** C2B

### Scenario E

A software company provides a system to a government department.

**Answer:** B2G

### Scenario F

A citizen submits an online government application.

**Answer:** C2G
